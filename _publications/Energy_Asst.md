### Abstract
Energy insecurity remains a pressing challenge for older adults, especially those aging in place with fixed incomes and deteriorating housing conditions. Using data from the 2020 Residential Energy Consumption Survey administered by the U.S. Energy Information Administration and inverse probability weighting with the average treatment effect to estimate causal effects, the study finds that recipients of energy bill assistance remain significantly more likely to experience energy related hardships. This indicates that assistance primarily reaches those already in crisis rather than preventing insecurity. Subgroup analyses show the strongest effects among homeowners in family houses, with limited impact among renters and mobile home residents. Environmental buffers such as shade trees offer modest protective effects. Based on these insights, the study recommends comprehensive reform of energy bill assistance programs, including transitioning from one time grants to monthly benefits aligned with utility billing cycles, increasing the value of existing grants, expanding outreach to underserved housing groups, integrating program delivery with utility communication systems, and investing in green infrastructure.
