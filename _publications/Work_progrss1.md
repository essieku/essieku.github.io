excerpt: |-
  Drawing on my job market findings, adults diagnosed early with ADHD who are now in their prime working years show a striking pattern: better health outcomes, higher educational attainment, more hours worked, and higher earnings, while other age groups often work more yet earn less. This study uses AI driven (unsupervised) clustering to learn what sets these prime age earners apart across observable domains, medication adherence, daily routines, family structure, and workplace supports, and translates those patterns into clear, generalizable policy guidance that can lift outcomes for the broader ADHD population.
